# Release note: DiamondBench, second run (for Jacob to post; no em dashes)

In July we published DiamondBench: 47 questions a diamond shopper actually asks, an answer key
with a source and date on every fact, a deterministic grader, and every raw answer, all open on
GitHub. This week we re-ran it against the same search-grounded model.

The score did not move. 95.7% both times, 45 of 47, and no answer in either run asserted
something false. The two misses are the same two. The three-stone ring's "past, present, future"
meaning is presented as tradition without its origin (a De Beers advertising campaign around
2000). The fluorescence answer reaches the right conclusion without the GIA study that supports
it. Seven weeks apart, the gemology is right and the provenance is still missing.

Two things changed under the hood, and we are saying so. The first scoring pass of the new run
read 85.1%. Auditing every miss against the raw answers, which the methodology requires, showed
that five of the seven were our grader, not the model: a forbidden keyword that also appears in a
true sentence, a bullet list that lost its "what to avoid" heading, and three answers that were
right in different words. We fixed the rules, re-graded July under the same rules (it did not
change), and published the regrade script so anyone can reproduce the audit.

One number we will keep watching: in July, stienhardt.com was the first source cited for the
Dutch Marquise definition. In September it is fifth of seven. The geometry is still right. Whose
page gets the credit is moving.
